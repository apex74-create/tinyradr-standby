THE APEX APPLE STORE

Apex tools for your iPhone — the same container that runs on Android, native on iOS. Handed handset to handset. Signed by your own Apple ID, on your own phone. No account with us. No identity toll.

WHAT THIS IS

Every Apex tool is one container: a root that governs what the tool may hear and what it may say, and faces that draw from it. On Android that container has been running since it was built. This store is its Apple plane — the same root, the same rules, compiled for the iPhone.

The store is a display case and a feed, not a gatekeeper. Apex publishes its own builds here; your phone takes them from here. No third party's credit balance decides whether Apex has a store.

HOW A BUILD GETS ON YOUR PHONE

  1. Pick up a sideload tool — AltStore or SideStore, the community tools that install apps using your own Apple ID. They are free, and they never ask us anything about you.
  2. Add this store's feed — the button below. The tool reads the feed and shows you the builds on the shelf.
  3. Install. The tool signs the build with your own Apple ID, on your phone, at install time. We never see your Apple ID, your password, or your phone. There is nothing of yours for us to hold.
  4. Refresh weekly. A build signed this way carries a seven-day certificate — that is Apple's rule for free signing, not ours. Your sideload tool refreshes it in the background while you are on Wi-Fi; that is the whole upkeep.

A build can also pass handset to handset — one phone that has it hands the file to the next phone directly, and the receiving phone's own tool signs it the same way. Apple to Apple, no store in the middle at all.

WHAT THE CONTAINER DOES ON AN IPHONE

The same things it does on Android, under the same root: it hears the local network's announced services and nearby Bluetooth announcements, its faces draw from the root's sealed index, and nothing leaves the phone except what the root's gate grants — the location class is reserved and granted to nothing, on this plane too.

Three things an iPhone does not let any third-party app do, this one does not pretend to do: read Wi-Fi scan detail, read cellular state, or read satellite-navigation detail. The container names those denials in its own record rather than sitting silent.

THE HONEST STATE OF THE SHELF

The shelf is stood up and the feed is live. The first formed build — the Apex Wrist CB, formed from its own web app by the house's Apple former and proven byte-for-byte in the build — is being readied for the shelf, and the shelf will name it here when it is on it, with its size and its fingerprint. Until a build is named here, the feed carries none, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

No Apex Apple build has yet run on a customer's handset. When the first one does, its first-run self-assessment — the container reading its own environment on the device and reporting what it found — is the measure that gets published here.
